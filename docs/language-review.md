# Language review: student-facing summaries and titles

Scope: every `summary` in religion_profiles (219), diffusions (117), exemplars (55) and events (55), plus every event `title` (55), in `data/build/africa.json`. Each flagged summary was checked against its own `detail` and `confidence`. Rules applied: `writing_rules` in `data/taxonomy.yaml` and the perspective rules in `docs/research-brief.md`.

Problem key: **1** banned or implied framing (generic "old/traditional" religion, "tribe", conversion as progress, Swahili towns as Arab colonies, oral tradition as legend). **2** outsider's claim stated as fact without attribution. **3** more certain than the record's own detail or confidence. **4** too hard for a 10th grader (unexplained term, very long sentence) or more than 2 sentences. **5** em dash.

## Patterns

1. **"Old" as the name of a religion.** The Kano and Kanem records lean on "the old religion," "old royal religion," "old cult," "old shrines," "old rites," "old powers." That is the generic label the brief bans in another form, and it quietly sets these religions up as the past and Islam as the future. Almost every one of these records has a specific name in its detail (Tsumburbura, Tchibiri, Santolo, the mune, Komo, nyama). Use the name, or "Kano's shrine religion," "Kanem's royal religion," "Mande ancestral religion."
2. **Religions defined by what they are not.** "Non-Muslim neighbors," "non-Muslim Mande artisans," "mostly non-Muslim Wolof farmers," and Damot's rulers who "did not follow Christianity or Islam." Fine as a description of how the Arabic sources saw people, but as the main label it makes Islam the measuring stick. State what people did practice where the detail allows it.
3. **Chronicle and tradition stated as event.** The most common problem. Records whose detail says "Chronicle (1880s compilation)," "Egharevba's chronology," "tradition, not a tested date," "dates range 1180-1330" often have summaries that state the event flatly in the past tense (Kano, Benin, Lalibela, Gao, Djenne, Sunni independence, Mossi sack, Jolof). Students lose exactly the historiography the map is for. A short source tag ("The Kano Chronicle says...", "Edo tradition says...", "dates range from...") fixes most of these without adding a sentence.
4. **Projection from later ethnography not signposted.** Great Zimbabwe, Benin, Bono, Mutapa and Hausa rural profiles describe religious content that the detail says comes from 20th-century practice. The summaries present it as 1200-1450 fact. Add "later evidence suggests" or "probably."
5. **Outsiders mostly attributed, with gaps.** Ibn Battuta and al-Umari are named in most summaries, which is good. The gaps are al-Bakri on Ghana and Malal, al-Umari on Musa's gold and the Kanem "Maliki" label, Ibn Khaldun on Mansa Uli, and Ibn Battuta's framing of Kilwa's raids, Mombasa, and Sa'id of Mogadishu. Also watch for attributed claims followed by an unhedged conclusion ("It is the clearest evidence that..."), which turns the outsider's report back into fact.
6. **Low-confidence records written like high-confidence ones.** Many `conf: low` records (Kano 1390-1410, Air, Mossi sack, Takrur rural, Malindi, Jolof) read the same as `conf: high` ones. The map shows confidence elsewhere, but the summary is what students read and remember.
7. **Unexplained terms.** Most are fine once glossed: Maliki/Shafi'i (a school of Islamic law), mahdi, Ibadi, Amazigh/Zanata, vizier, eparch, mihrab, patrician, Sarki, mallam, Ooni, orisa, regalia. Many are already glossed in some records and not others (madrasa, qadi). Gloss on first use in each summary, since students meet records one at a time.
8. **Colonial framings repeated without the rebuttal.** `ex-gedi-dated-tomb` repeats "Arab city" and leaves it there. `ex-great-enclosure` gets the pattern right (claim, then the evidence that overturned it). Make every colonial label do the same.
9. **Superlatives beyond the evidence.** "Largest ancient stone structure," "earliest recorded royal conversion," "the oldest direct written evidence," "most popular saint," "greatest center of Sunni learning." Several are fine; others go past what the detail supports (Takrur, Ife florescence, Cairo). Prefer "one of the earliest/largest" unless the detail backs a first or a largest.
10. **Out of scope but worth noting.** Four source titles contain "Pagan" or "Arab City" (`src-gamst-qemant`, `src-solomon-gebreyes-practices`, `src-welsby-medieval-kingdoms-nubia`, `src-kirkman-gedi`). They are bibliographic titles and must stay as published. If the app shows source titles to students, a one-line note on the source card (e.g. "title uses an outsider label") would turn them into teaching moments.
